Every beginner in this field goes through the same rite of passage — writing a “hello, world” program by hand.

![hello, world](./banner.webp)

## Where does "hello, world" come from?

As far as I can tell, it first appeared in Kernighan's internal technical memo _A Tutorial Introduction to the Language B_. After C replaced B, Kernighan wrote a more modern-looking version in another memo: `printf("hello, world");`. Later, _The C Programming Language_ by Kernighan and Ritchie was published. The book sold extremely well, and its first example program printed “hello, world”, which cemented its place as the first lesson of programming.

```c
int main() {
    printf("hello, world");
    return 0;
}
```

## What does "hello, world" mean to me?

Back when I first touched a computer, I also wondered why it had to be “hello, world” and not “hello, java” or “hello, javascript”. Years later I think “hello, world” is simply better, for two reasons:

1. The information technology boom was the third industrial revolution. By printing “hello, world”, a beginner signals to the field and to the world that new energy is arriving — there is something powerful and alive about that.
2. Every direction in this field depends heavily on the environment. Getting “hello, world” to print usually means the basics are set up, so you can stop fighting your toolchain and start learning.
